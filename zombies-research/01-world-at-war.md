# World at War Zombies

**Era:** Aether story — Original Timeline foundations

World at War begins as an unexplained survival mode and gradually becomes the foundation of the Aether story. Nacht der Untoten supplies almost no direct exposition; Verrückt adds environmental hints; Shi No Numa names the recurring cast and establishes a major Element 115 site; Der Riese turns scattered clues into a connected account of Group 935, teleportation, the undead programme, Samantha Maxis, and Edward Richtofen. **[Confirmed]**[^1][^2]

The official 2017 Zombies Chronicles timeline retroactively fixes many dates and relationships that were only implied when these maps first appeared. Where a point below depends on that later timeline rather than the 2008–09 map alone, it is identified as retrospective confirmation. **[Dev-stated]**[^3][^4]

## Nacht der Untoten

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** The original World at War version has no narrative main quest and no completion cutscene. Its objective is survival.
- **[Confirmed]** Small secrets—including the musical radio in later versions—are side interactions, not a story Easter egg comparable to later quests.[^5]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** Nacht has no bespoke intro or ending cinematic. The World at War campaign credits transition into the map, leaving the bunker assault unexplained inside the level itself.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** There are no collectible radios, documents, or formal intel entries in the original map. Its story information is environmental: a ruined airfield bunker, the undead outside, and the player’s isolation.
- **[Theory]** Early players read the hanging bodies, occult markings, and campaign setting as evidence of a Nazi experiment. That interpretation became broadly compatible with later Aether lore, but Nacht itself does not explicitly explain the outbreak.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** No encoded story cipher is attached to the original World at War release.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** The original player characters are unnamed American Marines. Ultimis only replaces them in later remastered versions; that substitution should not be treated as proof that Ultimis canonically fought the original Nacht survival event.[^6]

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Nacht is the first playable Zombies location and the least narratively specified World at War map.
- **[Confirmed]** Later material associates the wider era with Element 115 outbreaks and Group 935 activity, but the map contains no direct statement identifying who caused this particular incident.

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The exact canonical identities and fate of the Marines remain unresolved.
- **[Theory]** Attempts to identify the bunker’s outbreak as a specific Group 935 test should be presented as reconstruction, not something confirmed by an in-map recording.

## Verrückt

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** Verrückt has no completable narrative main quest. Restoring power joins the two starting wings and enables the map’s perk machines, but it does not produce an ending.
- **[Confirmed]** The dental drill, screaming ambience, blood messages, and interactive toilets are environmental or audio secrets rather than a formal story quest.[^7]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The map has no intro or ending cinematic. Its premise is communicated through the divided spawn and the ruined Wittenau Sanatorium.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Environmental evidence presents a violent institutional collapse: patient cells, surgery equipment, barricades, blood writing, and an electrified hand fixed to the power switch.
- **[Confirmed]** Retrospective Aether material identifies Verrückt with a failed extraction involving Peter McCain and an American team sent into the asylum. The named team and mission are much clearer in later radios and the official timeline than in the original map.[^4][^7]

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The World at War version contains no solved alternate-alphabet narrative cipher comparable to the Black Ops III or Black Ops 4 cipher sets.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** The original playable squad consists of unnamed Marines, commonly distinguished in game files/community documentation as Dempsey, Smokey, Banana, and Gunner; these labels are not an on-screen canonical character introduction.
- **[Confirmed]** Peter McCain is the central referenced intelligence figure in the later reconstruction of the operation, while Tank Dempsey’s capture becomes part of the official timeline’s account of the mission.[^4]

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** The asylum was touched by the early undead programme and became the site of a disastrous Allied incursion.
- **[Confirmed]** The map’s original release intentionally leaves the operation incomplete: there is no extraction, victory state, or cinematic resolution.

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The identity of every body and every message in the asylum cannot be assigned confidently. Later timeline knowledge makes a Peter/Dempsey reading plausible, but it does not turn every prop into an individually sourced event.

## Shi No Numa

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** Shi No Numa has no narrative main quest or ending. The telephone-triggered song and several radio interactions are side Easter eggs.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** Three radios appear in the original World at War map. Later remasters add further messages, which must be catalogued separately from the 2009 source set.[^8]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** There is no intro or ending cinematic. The four named characters are introduced through gameplay dialogue rather than a film.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** The map establishes a Japanese swamp facility linked to Division 9 activity and a substantial Element 115 meteor deposit.
- **[Confirmed]** The radios connect Cornelius Pernell, Peter McCain, and the search for the missing American operative. Peter’s hanging body is generally identified as the corpse visible in the spawn room, though the map does not display a written name beside it.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** The environment and messages expand the conspiracy beyond a purely German project: multiple governments and research groups are now pursuing 115.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** No substantial encoded text puzzle is required to understand the original World at War map. Its hidden narrative is delivered primarily through radios and environmental details.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Shi No Numa is the first map to present Edward Richtofen, Tank Dempsey, Nikolai Belinski, and Takeo Masaki together as the recurring four-person cast later named Ultimis.
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen already understands more about 115 and the facilities than he tells the others; his companions’ quotes establish suspicion and hostility without yet explaining his complete plan.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** The operation moves the cast through a facility associated with a 115 deposit and Peter McCain’s failed mission.
- **[Confirmed]** The official timeline later places the Ultimis group on the path from Shi No Numa to the Der Riese facility.[^4]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The exact order in which the squad searches the huts and activates every radio is gameplay-dependent; it should not be converted into a rigid canonical sequence.
- **[Theory]** Early claims that every supernatural event in the swamp was controlled directly by Samantha overstate what the original map alone confirms.

## Der Riese

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** Der Riese predates the full cinematic main-quest format. Its closest formal quest is the Fly Trap: activate Pack-a-Punch by linking the three teleporters, use an upgraded weapon to trigger the hidden control panel, then locate and shoot the concealed toys/items that appear around the facility.[^9]
- **[Confirmed]** Completing the Fly Trap awards the “Elevate Your Senses” achievement/trophy in the original-era release, but it does not end the match or deliver a narrative ending film.[^9]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** Der Riese has no bespoke intro or ending cinematic. The story is reconstructed from the facility, loading imagery, character dialogue, radios, and later timeline material.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Scattered radios document Group 935’s attempt to develop teleportation, weapons, and an undead army using Element 115.[^2][^10]
- **[Confirmed]** The recordings establish Maxis as programme leader, Richtofen as his increasingly disloyal subordinate, and Samantha as Maxis’s daughter. They also connect the test subject Fluffy, the teleporter, and the creation of the Hellhounds.[^10]
- **[Confirmed]** The critical accident occurs when Maxis orders Richtofen to resolve Samantha and Fluffy’s presence in the teleporter. Richtofen locks Maxis and Samantha in with the dog and activates the machine; their disappearance becomes the immediate precondition for Samantha’s supernatural role and Richtofen’s attempted takeover.[^4][^10]
- **[Confirmed]** The facility contains Group 935 insignia, animal-testing laboratories, teleporters, the Pack-a-Punch machine, and evidence of mass weapons development.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The original map uses coded-looking boards, scraps, coordinates, scientific notation, and hidden writing, but it does not contain a single mandatory cipher whose solution completes the Fly Trap.[^11]
- **[Confirmed]** Some transcribed scraps repeat or paraphrase radio material. Later remakes add new ciphers and should not be silently backdated to World at War.
- **[Theory]** Community attempts to extract additional messages from every number string produced many speculative readings. Only solutions supported by a repeatable method and a stable plaintext should be treated as solved.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Ultimis arrives at the facility with Richtofen pursuing his own agenda. Dempsey, Nikolai, and Takeo are former test subjects whose fragmented memories and hostility are expressed through quotes and later documentation.
- **[Confirmed]** Ludwig Maxis, Samantha Maxis, Sophia, and Fluffy appear through recordings or evidence rather than as playable characters.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Der Riese is the point where the first game’s isolated outbreaks become a traceable conspiracy centred on Group 935 and Element 115.
- **[Confirmed]** The official timeline identifies 28 October 1945 as the date Ultimis returns to Der Riese and overloads the teleporter, sending the group forward to Kino der Toten.[^4][^12]
- **[Dev-stated]** Treyarch’s 2017 timeline formalized the multimap history and described itself as a detailed account of the Zombies universe; it is therefore stronger evidence for chronology than release-era fan ordering.[^3]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The Fly Trap’s toys are often interpreted as symbolic anchors for Samantha. The association is thematically strong, but the side quest does not provide a direct explanatory statement.
- **[Theory]** Debate over whether particular World at War visual details were planned as long-term canon should remain separate from what later games explicitly canonized.

## World at War synthesis

- **[Confirmed]** There is no conventional “main Easter egg completion” for the first three maps, and Der Riese’s Fly Trap is a side quest rather than an ending-producing story mission.
- **[Confirmed]** The evidentiary progression is deliberate: minimal context at Nacht, institutional horror at Verrückt, named characters and international 115 activity at Shi No Numa, then a dense Group 935 archive at Der Riese.
- **[Theory]** A seamless moment-by-moment narrative connecting every World at War survival match is a later reconstruction. The safest archive distinguishes original in-map evidence from chronology added by the Zombies Chronicles timeline and later Aether entries.
