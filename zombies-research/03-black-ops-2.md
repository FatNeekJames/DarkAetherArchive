# Black Ops II Zombies

**Era:** Aether story — fractured Earth, Victis towers, the Mob cycle, and Dimension 63

Black Ops II deliberately tells three connected but structurally different stories. TranZit, Die Rise and Buried follow Victis across the ruined Original Timeline while Maxis and Richtofen compete for global polarization towers. Mob of the Dead depicts a supernatural Alcatraz cycle whose eventual disruption becomes essential to the multiverse. Origins moves to Dimension 63 in 1918 and introduces the younger Primis counterparts who drive Black Ops III. **[Confirmed]**[^1][^2]

The two selectable tower paths are both playable, but later story material treats Maxis's Buried victory as the branch that leads Samantha to Dimension 63 and Origins. The opposing Richtofen outcome is documented below as a real alternate quest conclusion, not silently merged into the canonical continuation. **[Confirmed]**[^1][^3]

## TranZit / Green Run

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Tower of Babble” asks Victis to energize the Green Run pylon for either Maxis or Richtofen. Both routes require the Original difficulty and build toward mutually opposed uses of the same infrastructure.[^4]
- **[Confirmed]** The shared setup is to travel the bus route, assemble equipment, reach the power station and hear both voices. Maxis asks the crew to shut power down because it obstructs his signal; Richtofen asks Stuhlinger to keep power available and channel energy for him.[^4][^5]
- **[Confirmed]** On Maxis's route, the crew turns power off, draws the electrical being Avogadro beneath the pylon, defeats it with Electromagnetic Pulse grenades, and powers lamps with Turbines so their energy converges on the tower. On Richtofen's route, the crew keeps power active, uses the Jet Gun beneath the tower until it breaks, then coordinates EMP detonations at distant green streetlights.[^4]
- **[Confirmed]** Either completion changes the tower/map indicator to the patron's colour and records progress toward Buried's Endgame. It does not end the match.[^4]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The opening cinematic presents four survivors travelling through the post-Moon wasteland and converging around the bus system. The scene establishes distrust, scarcity and Stuhlinger's ability to hear Richtofen without disclosing his cannibal past in full.
- **[Confirmed]** There is no ending cinematic. Maxis or Richtofen confirms the tower's polarization by voice, and the altered pylon remains as the result.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** The Farm television broadcasts Maxis's fragmented explanation that the Moon calculations threatened the globe and that polarization devices may provide a remedy; power fluctuations interrupt him.[^6]
- **[Confirmed]** Additional TV messages show that other survivor groups were contacted before Victis and failed to complete the work. This makes Victis one of multiple attempted pawns rather than uniquely chosen heroes.[^5][^6]
- **[Confirmed]** Environmental remains at the Bus Depot, Diner, Farm, power station and Town show a civil-defence landscape destroyed by the missile strikes, lava fissures and subsequent undead collapse.
- **[Confirmed]** Stuhlinger alone hears Richtofen clearly because consuming zombie flesh linked him to the Aether controller; the rest initially hear only Maxis and must decide whether to trust Stuhlinger's private instructions.[^5]

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** TranZit's main puzzle language is electrical and spatial: buildable parts, bus-route navigation, EMP timing, streetlight teleportation and tower geometry. It contains no major long-form ciphertext archive comparable to Mob or Origins.
- **[Confirmed]** The Navcard system is deliberately cross-map: the cards and tables only make full sense when TranZit, Die Rise and Buried are treated as one linked network.[^7]

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Samuel Stuhlinger, Abigail “Misty” Briarton, Marlton Johnson and Russman form Victis. Their alliance is practical and uneasy.
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen speaks privately to Stuhlinger from the Aether; Maxis addresses the group through electronics. The Avogadro is both a boss-like electrical entity and the energy source Maxis's route exploits.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Earth survived Moon only as a shattered, contaminated environment. Maxis admits the destructive calculation did not produce the intended clean result.[^5][^6]
- **[Confirmed]** Both Aether powers need human agents to manipulate physical towers. Their apparent omnipotence is therefore constrained by access to living intermediaries and working machinery.

### Theories

- **[Theory]** Many small environmental details have been used to reconstruct exactly how every Green Run settlement fell. Unless tied to a readable message or later timeline entry, those sequences remain plausible local histories rather than confirmed minute-by-minute accounts.
- **[Theory]** The Avogadro's precise relationship to later Broken Arrow experiments was underdetermined in Black Ops II and clarified only by later maps; interpretations should be dated to their evidence source.

## Die Rise

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “High Maintenance” continues the two-path tower contest in a collapsed Shanghai skyscraper complex. Four players are required for the original full quest.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** The shared route includes building and using the Nav table, activating elevator symbols, standing on the four golden floor emblems, and energizing the radio tower. Richtofen's branch uses the Sliquifier and coordinated Trample Steam placement; Maxis's branch uses upgraded Ballistic Knives and distinct energy instructions.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** Both branches finish by reading Mahjong tiles to derive an ordered set of compass directions, then punching the tower's four corners with Galvaknuckles in that order. The tower glows blue for Richtofen or orange for Maxis and all players receive the map's six perks.[^8]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The intro shows Victis repeatedly dying and returning, with Richtofen instructing Stuhlinger to mend the rift while Maxis's influence competes for the group. It explicitly makes resurrection and memory fragmentation part of their journey rather than a purely noncanonical game reset.
- **[Confirmed]** No ending film follows the tower quest; environmental colour and voice confirmation record the chosen allegiance.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Television messages report airborne contamination, memory loss, psychosis and paranoia; a stranded woman describes failing instructions from “the German man”; a Flesh preacher advocates consuming the undead; and a military transmission describes a failed sweep of a compromised Broken Arrow site.[^9]
- **[Confirmed]** These broadcasts connect Victis's symptoms and Stuhlinger's Flesh affiliation to a wider survivor crisis. They also identify Broken Arrow as an American programme whose records were being destroyed during the outbreak.[^9]
- **[Confirmed]** The location's Chinese signage, displaced buildings and vertical ruin show the planetary event's global reach rather than a crisis limited to North America.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The final Mahjong solution is a genuine decoding task. Four Mahjong tiles indicate an ordering and compass orientation; players translate that information into the North/South/East/West order for the tower corners.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** The repeated dragon/elevator symbols are quest state markers. Trial-and-error can reproduce some steps, but community comparison of tile values and directional markings explains the intended method.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Victis remains the playable group. Stuhlinger is increasingly isolated by Richtofen's voice and by partial memories of prior deaths.
- **[Confirmed]** Maxis and Richtofen continue acting as competing mission controllers. The anonymous TV speakers provide evidence of survivor cells, the Flesh and Broken Arrow beyond the playable crew.[^9]

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** The crew's journey is cyclic enough that the intro shows prior failure, yet the tower completion persists as global-map state. Gameplay reset and story recurrence deliberately overlap.
- **[Confirmed]** Broken Arrow is implicated in pre-collapse research and containment activity, a thread Black Ops 4 later expands substantially.[^9]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** It is not possible to assign every corpse or abandoned room to a named TV speaker. The broadcasts establish regional stories without mapping each speaker to a prop.
- **[Theory]** The mechanism that preserves some quest progress across Victis deaths is left metaphysical; Richtofen's control and the rift are likely involved, but Die Rise does not give a technical explanation.

## Mob of the Dead

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** The immediate escape quest, “No One Escapes Alive,” has the prisoners enter Afterlife, power the prison, collect aircraft parts, build Icarus on the roof and fly toward the Golden Gate Bridge—only to crash and awaken back on Alcatraz.[^10]
- **[Confirmed]** “Pop Goes the Weasel” turns that failed escape into the main ending: repeat the flight, retrieve the Afterlife headphones/audio logs by entering the inmates' prison numbers, return to the roof in Afterlife, board the spectral plane and reach the bridge for a final confrontation.[^10][^11]
- **[Confirmed]** Sal, Finn and Billy become hostile to Arlington. If they kill the Weasel, “The Cycle Continues”; if Arlington kills the other three, “The Cycle Is Broken.” Blood of the Dead later confirms the cycle-breaking outcome as the consequential branch.[^10]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The intro shows the four mobsters attempting Arlington's improvised prison escape on New Year's Eve 1933. Their plan collapses into violence, the undead overrun Alcatraz, and death returns them to the beginning.
- **[Confirmed]** The ending is an interactive execution rather than a separate long film. The result text and voiceover identify whether the cycle repeats or breaks, making player-versus-player action part of the narrative resolution.[^10]

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Stanley Ferguson's hidden audio logs recount the official version of Arlington's escape plan and the mobsters' deaths. The logs are unlocked by entering the four prisoners' identification numbers into the Citadel counter.[^11]
- **[Confirmed]** Later material reveals that Ferguson recorded the account in 1943 at “Mr. Rapt's” request, connecting the Shadowman to the construction or maintenance of the pocket dimension.[^11]
- **[Confirmed]** Newspaper clippings, prison records, Arlington's sketches and repeated visual tableaux contrast the mundane 1933 crime with the supernatural purgatory layered over it.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The map contains five ciphers plus a loading-screen cipher and a reconstructable paper drawing.[^12]
- **[Confirmed]** A key solution uses a 41-character Hill cipher. The 4×4 encoding matrix is embedded in the loading screen, and the key “MOBOFTHEDEADABCD” produces plaintext about Sal's criminal alliance and the consequences of the 1929 St Valentine's Day Massacre.[^12]
- **[Confirmed]** Other solved texts expand the inmates' criminal history and Arlington's scheme. Their placement and keys demonstrate that the comic/loading artwork is part of the evidentiary surface, not merely decoration.
- **[Theory]** Early community transcriptions differed where low-resolution textures obscured characters. A solution should be treated as stable only when re-encoding or another reproducible check matches the map text.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Albert “The Weasel” Arlington, Salvatore DeLuca, Billy Handsome and Michael “Finn” O'Leary are the playable prisoners. Stanley Ferguson is the guard/narrator; Brutus/the Warden enforces the prison cycle.
- **[Confirmed]** Arlington designed Icarus and was murdered by the other three in the underlying historical event. The purgatory repeatedly restages betrayal until the Weasel defeats them.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Mob occurs in a closed pocket dimension where death, Afterlife and the failed flight repeat. It is not simply a normal Alcatraz outbreak.[^10]
- **[Confirmed]** Breaking the cycle has multiversal consequences: it later removes the protective mechanism that had trapped Primis in Blood of the Dead and permits the Great War Richtofen's intervention.

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The exact ontological label—purgatory, pocket dimension, hell—is expressed differently by characters and community summaries. “Cycle-bound pocket dimension” most conservatively describes the confirmed mechanics.
- **[Theory]** Not every supernatural object is attributable directly to the Shadowman. Later evidence confirms his manipulation but does not provide a manufacturing history for each prop.

## Buried

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Mined Games” is the third tower quest. Richtofen asks Victis to build and power a guillotine; Maxis asks for a gallows. Both use the haunted western town's buildables, switches, targets and energy systems to connect the final polarization device.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** In summary, the Richtofen branch constructs the guillotine, charges it through wisp/orb interactions, uses the Time Bomb to coordinate switches and finishes with a four-player target-shooting sequence. The Maxis branch constructs the gallows, destroys energy orbs, uses the Subsurface Resonator and lantern/cipher sequence, rings the correct bells and likewise finishes by synchronizing the town's mechanisms.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** The separate Endgame becomes available only when all four players have completed Tower of Babble, High Maintenance and Mined Games for the same side and inserted the linked Navcards. The courthouse tracker verifies the stored state.[^3][^7]
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen's Endgame erases Maxis and begins taking over Stuhlinger's body. Maxis's Endgame takes control of the Aether, casts Richtofen into a zombie and announces that opening Agartha will doom Earth while reuniting him with Samantha.[^3]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The intro follows Victis falling into the subterranean town near the rift, presenting an impossible Old West settlement displaced beneath an Angolan mining site.
- **[Confirmed]** There is no pre-rendered quest ending. Competing voice exchanges, global colour changes and gameplay effects communicate the selected Endgame.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** The town, mansion, mine and rift provide environmental evidence of temporal/spatial displacement. The settlement is not naturally located in modern Angola.
- **[Confirmed]** Maxis and Richtofen's instructions reveal their actual stakes more clearly than before: each seeks sole Aether control, and neither offers Victis a safe, transparent plan.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** Arthur, commonly called Leroy by the community, is physically present and helps move obstacles, hold crawlers and operate the town when treated with candy or alcohol; the map does not give him that fan nickname as a formal identity.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** Maxis's lantern step produces a readable coded message that directs players through the sign/maze sequence; the intended solution depends on collecting and interpreting environmental symbols rather than guessing the final switches.
- **[Confirmed]** The bell locations, courthouse tracker, target gallery and Navcard network are distributed cooperative puzzles. The tracker is also a diegetic save-state display for three maps.[^3][^7]

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Victis is again playable. Maxis and Richtofen are the rival voices; Arthur is the settlement's surviving resident/helper; the mansion's spectral women guard the route to Pack-a-Punch.
- **[Confirmed]** Stuhlinger is the physical target of Richtofen's winning branch. In Maxis's branch, Richtofen is forced into successive undead bodies.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** The Maxis Endgame is the branch used by the later Aether narrative: Maxis reaches Agartha and Samantha subsequently seeks help in Dimension 63.[^1][^3]
- **[Confirmed]** Buried concludes the Victis tower campaign but not the characters' existence; later entries reveal that they are preserved for another task.

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The town's exact path from the American West to Angola is only partially explained through later dimensional history. Treating every architectural anomaly as a separate teleport event exceeds the direct evidence.
- **[Theory]** Neither Endgame should be called a morally “good” ending. Maxis is the continuity-leading choice, but his own speech anticipates the destruction of remaining life.

## Origins

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Little Lost Girl” follows Primis as Samantha guides them through the excavation and asks to be freed. Unlike the Victis tower quests, it can be completed solo as well as cooperatively.[^13]
- **[Confirmed]** The summarized eight-stage sequence mirrors Reznov's Vorkuta imperatives: secure and upgrade all four elemental staffs; place them in the giant robots and excavation pedestals; use a robot's purge control and a G-Strike to break the seal; deploy the Maxis Drone into the opened pit; defeat the released Panzer Soldats; recover the upgraded Drone while in Zombie Blood; use the One Inch Punch to free glowing-arm zombies; place the staffs in the Crazy Place and charge the portal with souls; then send the Drone into the portal to trigger the ending.[^13][^14]
- **[Confirmed]** The quest transforms Samantha's rescue plea into a portal out of the excavation. The crew follows, beginning Primis's larger multiversal mission.

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The stylized intro introduces Dimension 63's younger Richtofen, Dempsey, Takeo and Nikolai converging on Excavation Site 64 during the First World War. Richtofen has already removed Maxis's brain after 115-induced madness.
- **[Confirmed]** The ending pulls back to Samantha and Eddie playing with figures in a bedroom while Maxis calls them to close the window. On its 2013 release the scene supported multiple “it was a game” readings; Black Ops III later locates the house and children inside a real multiversal/Agarthan framework rather than erasing prior events as imagination.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Six original Maxis radios cover replica Amplification Rods, 115-created portals, the gramophone opening ancient chambers, attacks by ancient figures, future weapons appearing through the Mystery Box, and the mysterious child claiming to be his daughter.[^15]
- **[Confirmed]** A seventh Doctor Monty radio is exclusive to the Black Ops III remaster and is later commentary, not part of the 2013 Origins archive.[^15]
- **[Confirmed]** The dig site combines Group 935 research, ancient elemental technology and objects pulled across time. This establishes Dimension 63 as a different history rather than a simple prequel to Ultimis.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** Origins contains five ciphers and one illustrated scrap. The first three are Vigenère texts using the key **wewerethereatthebeginningandattheend**.[^16]
- **[Confirmed]** Their plaintexts describe Maxis's growing mistrust of Edward, awe at the Ancients' knowledge, Edward's early brilliance and Maxis's contact with the Allies. The fifth ends with the Latin instruction *Liberate tute de infernis*—“free yourself from hell.”[^16]
- **[Confirmed]** The staff upgrades are also formal decoding puzzles: players translate elemental symbols, musical/positional patterns and environmental dials before charging each weapon in the Crazy Place.
- **[Confirmed]** Community solvers combined frequency/pattern analysis, a discoverable thematic key and successful re-encoding. The explicit key and coherent multi-message result make these substantially stronger than visual-only theories.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Primis consists of the Dimension 63 versions of Edward Richtofen, Tank Dempsey, Takeo Masaki and Nikolai Belinski. They share identities with Ultimis but not the same histories or personalities.
- **[Confirmed]** Samantha speaks from beyond the map; Maxis survives as a brain mounted to the Maxis Drone; Eddie appears beside Samantha in the ending; Doctor Monty is only retroactively connected through later material.
- **[Confirmed]** Primis Richtofen already possesses knowledge beyond what he shares and immediately becomes the group's mission driver.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Origins occurs on 4 June 1918 at Excavation Site 64 in Dimension 63.[^2][^17]
- **[Confirmed]** Element 115 opens portals through space and time, allowing objects from other eras to enter the battlefield; the radios state this hypothesis and gameplay demonstrates it.[^15]
- **[Confirmed]** The map begins a new causal strand: Samantha's request recruits Primis, whose future interventions alter or fracture many events inherited from the Original Timeline.

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The 2013 ending was initially read by some players as confirmation that all Zombies events were children's play. Later games contradict that reductive interpretation by making the House, Samantha and Eddie components of the multiverse.
- **[Theory]** The exact creators and original purpose of every object in the Crazy Place were not fully explained in Black Ops II. Later Apothicon/Keeper cosmology should be identified as retrospective information when used.

## Black Ops II synthesis

- **[Confirmed]** Victis's tower trilogy presents a branching campaign with persistent account-level allegiance. Later continuity selects Maxis's victory, but the Richtofen route remains a fully authored alternative outcome.[^3][^7]
- **[Confirmed]** Mob and Origins are not diversions: breaking the Alcatraz cycle and forming Primis become prerequisites for the Black Ops III/4 resolution.
- **[Confirmed]** Black Ops II expands “intel” beyond radios. Television broadcasts, persistent world-map lights, loading-screen matrices, prison numbers and cross-map Navcards all carry story state.
- **[Theory]** Release order is not chronological order. A single linear list that places 1918 Origins after 2035 Buried without explaining dimensions misrepresents the narrative design.
