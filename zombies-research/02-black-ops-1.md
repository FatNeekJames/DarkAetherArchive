# Black Ops Zombies

**Era:** Aether story — Ultimis and Richtofen's Grand Scheme

Black Ops turns the environmental mystery of *World at War* into a linked quest narrative. Ultimis moves from Kino der Toten to Ascension, is displaced to the Siberian facility during the Call of the Dead incident, recovers the Vril Device and Focusing Stone, and finally reaches Griffin Station on the Moon. There, Richtofen replaces Samantha inside the Moon Pyramid Device; Maxis answers by directing rockets into Earth. **[Confirmed]**[^1][^2]

This file covers the five maps named in the research brief. *Five* and Dead Ops Arcade are deliberately outside this checkpoint's map list, although later material links some of their characters and locations to the wider story. **[Confirmed]**

## Kino der Toten

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** Kino has no narrative main quest and no match-ending Easter egg. The core story interaction is the film-reel system: activate the stage teleporter, enter one of four brief side rooms after leaving Pack-a-Punch, collect up to three reels, and play them through the theatre projector on later teleports.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** Shooting or otherwise activating the two original-release radios and interacting with the three 115 meteor fragments reveal additional audio and trigger the song “115”; these are side secrets rather than a quest chain.[^3]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The map has no bespoke intro or ending cinematic. Ultimis arrives by an overloaded Der Riese teleporter in 1963 Berlin, and the match continues until the players are overwhelmed.[^1][^4]
- **[Confirmed]** The loading image and playable environment, rather than a film, establish a Group 935 theatre used for projection and behavioural-conditioning experiments.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Three playable film reels document Maxis's attempt to programme test subjects through edited films and subconscious conditioning. Subject 26 initially follows basic instructions, then loses control during a field test and is destroyed.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** Two Black Ops radios extend that experiment: Maxis believes retention is improving, then records that flashing lights and a fire alarm coincided with Subject 26's breakdown.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** The four post-teleport rooms—two forms of Samantha's bedroom, a dentist's office and a conference room—function as fragmentary story spaces. They connect childhood trauma, Group 935 treatment, and the theatre's projection programme without establishing a literal itinerary through normal space.
- **[Confirmed]** The third radio commonly listed for Kino belongs to the Black Ops III Zombies Chronicles version and features Doctor Monty; it is not original Black Ops evidence.[^3]

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** Kino contains recurring numbers, letters, Group 935 imagery and rapidly edited frames, but no required, repeatably solved text cipher controls a narrative quest.
- **[Theory]** Players have proposed identities for Subject 26 and meanings for every flashed character. The recordings never name the subject, so identifying the patient as a particular playable character remains speculation.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Edward Richtofen, Tank Dempsey, Nikolai Belinski and Takeo Masaki are the playable Ultimis crew.
- **[Confirmed]** Ludwig Maxis and the unnamed Subject 26 appear through recordings; Samantha is evoked through the teleporter rooms and environmental imagery.
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen loses his diary during the unstable arrival, a detail that helps explain why later agents can acquire information he meant to keep private.[^1][^4]

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Kino was a Group 935 behavioural-research site, not merely an abandoned cinema.[^3]
- **[Confirmed]** The official Zombies timeline places Ultimis's accidental arrival on 28 October 1945 from their perspective, but the destination is 1963. The temporal jump is therefore explicit retrospective chronology rather than an inference from the theatre alone.[^1]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The precise mechanics of the random rooms—memory, Aether displacement, or physically distinct locations—are not explained by the original map.
- **[Theory]** Visual references to campaign brainwashing invite comparison with the Black Ops Numbers Programme, but the Zombies reels do not state that the two programmes are identical.

## Ascension

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Casimir Mechanism” is the first multi-stage cooperative main quest in the series. In the original Black Ops version it requires four players and culminates in freeing Gersh from the mechanism.[^5]
- **[Confirmed]** The summarized sequence is: restore power; activate the hidden generator with a Gersh Device; coordinate the lunar landers and pressure plate; launch the rocket; assemble and interpret the LUNA sequence through the lander terminals; use the clock/letter and Matryoshka-doll interactions; then direct upgraded weapon energy, a Gersh Device and Matryoshka Dolls into the final orb so Gersh can escape.[^5]
- **[Confirmed]** Completion grants the team an extended Death Machine reward and resolves Gersh's immediate imprisonment, but does not end the survival match or show an ending film.

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** Ascension has no bespoke intro or ending cinematic. Ultimis arrives at the Soviet cosmodrome through another teleportation event; the result of the quest is communicated by effects and Gersh's voice.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Six radios trace the Ascension Group's research and the conflict between Gersh and Yuri Zavoyski. Yuri grows resentful after being reassigned, is influenced by Samantha through a Matryoshka Doll, activates a device and traps Gersh.[^6]
- **[Confirmed]** The recordings tie Soviet research to captured Group 935 material and to Project Mercury/Gersh Device teleportation work. They also show that Samantha can manipulate vulnerable researchers rather than acting only through direct attacks.[^6]
- **[Confirmed]** The red telephones connect the Zombies space to the contemporaneous Black Ops campaign cast through short audio contacts, but they do not make Mason's team playable participants in the Ascension quest.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The letters entered through the lunar-lander terminals spell **LUNA**, a mechanical quest code rather than an encrypted lore paragraph.[^5]
- **[Confirmed]** The campaign's separate Ascension/Numbers cipher uses a keystream associated with *Profiles in Courage*. That is relevant context for the facility's Cold War iconography, but it should not be miscatalogued as a Zombies-map cipher required by Casimir Mechanism.[^7]

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Ultimis remains the playable crew.
- **[Confirmed]** Gersh is the trapped scientist asking for help; Yuri is his subordinate and betrayer under Samantha's influence. Samantha acts as the hostile Aether power opposing the crew and Gersh.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** The Ascension Group reproduced or extended teleportation and 115-derived research after the war.[^6]
- **[Confirmed]** Freeing Gersh provides a narrative bridge to later Aether appearances, while the malfunctioning mechanism is responsible for further displacement of Ultimis.[^1][^5]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** Because the reward does not end the game, the precise interval between freeing Gersh and Ultimis's disappearance is not playable chronology.
- **[Theory]** Telephone cameos are sometimes used to claim a direct meeting between Ultimis and the campaign team. The audio establishes contact or proximity, not a face-to-face meeting.

## Call of the Dead

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Stand-in” is the solo-compatible version and “Ensemble Cast” the longer cooperative version of the map's main quest. The celebrity crew helps Ultimis, who are trapped behind a sealed door after the Casimir Mechanism sends them too far into the future.[^8][^9]
- **[Confirmed]** The summarized common route is: restore power; speak to Richtofen and recover the fuse; repair power to the sealed room; destroy the external generators; enter the correct lighthouse/control-room settings; use the radios and foghorns in sequence to produce the submarine's green beam; transform a zombie with the V-R11 so it rises through that beam; then collect and deliver the Golden Rod/Vril Device.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** The cooperative route adds a vodka delivery and further coordination. Completion returns the trapped crew to their journey and leaves the celebrity cast a Wunderwaffe reward; it does not trigger a conventional ending cinematic.[^8]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** The live-action intro presents George A. Romero filming Sarah Michelle Gellar, Robert Englund, Michael Rooker and Danny Trejo at the Siberian location before an undead Romero attacks and the set becomes a real outbreak.[^9]
- **[Confirmed]** There is no ending movie. The quest's conclusion is heard through the door, followed by the crew's disappearance and an in-game reward.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Five of the nine scattered radios play sequential Richtofen diaries recorded in 1945; the others function as quest inputs.[^10]
- **[Confirmed]** The diaries record Richtofen's anger at Maxis's DG-2 programme, his concern about Samantha, and his experiments on Dempsey, Nikolai, Takeo and an unnamed Mexican test subject.[^10]
- **[Confirmed]** The Siberian facility therefore connects the playable cast to Richtofen's human experimentation before Shi No Numa and Der Riese made them a team.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The quest uses ordered dials, a wheel and levers, radio tones, foghorn patterns and the Morse code “VRIL” as layered code/puzzle inputs. These are solved environmental sequences, not a long substitution-cipher plaintext.[^8]
- **[Confirmed]** Community walkthrough work established a repeatable order by matching audio cues and environmental feedback. Different solo/co-op requirements account for many apparently conflicting early guides.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Sarah Michelle Gellar, Robert Englund, Michael Rooker and Danny Trejo are the playable fictionalized celebrity cast; George Romero is the persistent boss enemy.[^9]
- **[Confirmed]** Ultimis is present but physically hidden behind the door. Richtofen directs the recovery because he needs the Vril Device for the later Moon plan.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** The recovered Golden Rod is the Vril Device previously taken from the Siberian site; it becomes one of the two artefacts Richtofen needs to complete his Grand Scheme.[^1][^8]
- **[Confirmed]** The incident occurs in 2011, after Ascension in the crew's sequence but at a facility tied to Richtofen's 1945 experiments.[^1][^9]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** The intro deliberately blurs the real filmmaker and a fictional Zombies version of Romero. It should not be read as a claim about the real production's history.
- **[Theory]** The unnamed Mexican subject is often connected to later characters or replacement-crew ideas, but Black Ops does not confirm his identity.

## Shangri-La

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Time Travel Will Tell” requires four players in the original release and sends Ultimis repeatedly into an eclipse-state past to help the explorers Brock and Gary and obtain the Focusing Stone.[^11]
- **[Confirmed]** The summarized sequence is: restore power and press four eclipse switches together; communicate with the trapped explorers; match the floor symbols; trigger and shrink the meteor/crystal obstruction with the 31-79 JGb215; route the water-slide/pressure-plate interactions; release gas and lead a calm Napalm Zombie through it to ignite the line; plug wall openings with Spikemore-triggered zombies; set the correct dials; strike the gongs to identify the matching tones; charge the crystals, shoot the dynamite and return to the temple; then take the Focusing Stone.[^11]
- **[Confirmed]** Each successful phase changes the explorers' recorded message and returns the players to the present. The final event reveals that Brock and Gary remain caught in a loop around their discovery of the temple.[^11][^12]

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** Shangri-La has no bespoke intro or ending movie. The eclipse, shifting environment, radio dialogue and acquisition of the Focusing Stone perform the narrative role later maps give to cinematics.

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** The changing portable-radio messages follow Brock and Gary's expedition, attack by the undead, deaths and altered attempts at survival. The radio relocates and changes after quest steps because the crew is revising the explorers' path through time.[^12]
- **[Confirmed]** The site contains Element 115, Vril-related architecture and a temporal mechanism. The quest demonstrates time travel directly through repeatable state changes rather than merely implying it.

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** The floor-symbol step uses paired glyphs as a cooperative memory/matching puzzle; the gong step is solved by identifying the four resonant gongs that produce the correct tones; and wall dials must be set to a known numerical pattern.[^11]
- **[Confirmed]** These sequences were solved collaboratively through observation, trial comparison and audio/visual feedback. No stable long-form encrypted lore message is required for completion.
- **[Theory]** Asset names, eclipse imagery and the shape of the visible moon have supported the long-running “Shangri-La is on Mars” theory. The quest and radios never state this; community discussion remains divided.[^13]

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Ultimis is the playable crew. Brock and Gary are the explorers heard through the changing radio. Samantha remains the hostile controller of the undead.
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen seeks the Focusing Stone as the second major artefact required for his Moon plan.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Ultimis obtains the Focusing Stone, and Richtofen combines its power with the Vril Device in the next map.[^1][^11]
- **[Confirmed]** Brock and Gary's local sequence is a causal loop: aid changes individual stages, yet their expedition returns to the discovery point instead of producing a clean escape.[^11]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** Shangri-La's exact geographic and planetary status is intentionally contentious. The Himalayan-coordinate clue supports an Earth location; Mars readings rely on visual/asset evidence and later allusions rather than an explicit Black Ops statement.[^13]
- **[Theory]** The identity and origin of the temple builders are not fully resolved by this map; “Vril civilisation” is a descriptive community shorthand, not a complete confirmed ethnography.

## Moon

### Easter Egg

- **[Confirmed]** “Richtofen's Grand Scheme” comprises “Cryogenic Slumber Party” and the fuller “Big Bang Theory.” In the original Black Ops release, completing the entire chain requires Richtofen's player to possess the Golden Rod and Focusing Stone from the preceding quests.[^14]
- **[Confirmed]** The summarized first phase is: restore power; complete the colour-matching “Samantha Says” terminals; use the Hacker on the laboratory controls; allow Excavator Pi to breach Tunnel 6 and recover the Vril Sphere; guide that sphere through the base; open the Moon Pyramid Device and fill its four tubes with zombie souls; then place the charged artefact so Richtofen and Samantha exchange bodies.[^14]
- **[Confirmed]** The extended phase returns to Earth for the plates, charges them and the Vril Device, plays further terminal inputs, moves the sphere to the launch apparatus, and sends three 115-bearing rockets into Earth. The resulting explosions catastrophically fracture the planet.[^14][^15]
- **[Confirmed]** The event changes the Zombies announcer/eye colour to Richtofen and grants persistent gameplay rewards, but the match itself can continue.

### Cutscenes

- **[Confirmed]** Moon has no traditional pre-rendered intro or ending cinematic. Players begin at Groom Lake/Area 51 and teleport to Griffin Station; the soul exchange and Earth's destruction occur as live scripted events visible from the map.[^15]
- **[Confirmed]** Maxis speaks through the station's electronics after Richtofen's takeover and presents the rocket strike as a way to sever Richtofen's connection, although the strike devastates rather than saves Earth.[^14]

### Intel

- **[Confirmed]** Five original radios and six playable audio reels cover early Group 935 teleportation, Griffin Station's discovery and operation, the soul-powered Moon Pyramid Device, Samantha's arrival inside it, and Richtofen's instructions to Groph and Schuster.[^16]
- **[Confirmed]** An early log records the successful teleportation of a walnut, establishing the research line that leads to human transfer. Later reels reveal that souls fill the pyramid's canisters and that Samantha becomes trapped inside the device after the Der Riese incident.[^16]
- **[Confirmed]** The material shows that Griffin Station's staff continued obeying Richtofen while Maxis and Samantha were missing, then struggled to understand and contain the power inside the pyramid.
- **[Confirmed]** A sixth “radio” featuring Doctor Monty is exclusive to the Black Ops III remaster and must not be attributed to the 2011 evidence set.[^16]

### Ciphers

- **[Confirmed]** “Samantha Says” is a Simon-style colour-sequence puzzle. The Hacker terminals, excavator scheduling, sphere routing and artefact assembly form a machine-state puzzle rather than a classical alphabet cipher.[^14]
- **[Confirmed]** The password associated with Richtofen's terminal is presented through character dialogue and interface interaction. Community guides sometimes label the whole quest a cipher, but Moon does not contain a large solved wall ciphertext comparable to later Black Ops III maps.

### Characters

- **[Confirmed]** Ultimis completes the operation, but Richtofen and Samantha exchange controlling positions: Richtofen enters the MPD/Aether and Samantha occupies Richtofen's body.[^14][^15]
- **[Confirmed]** Ludwig Maxis survives as an electronic/Aether presence and directs Dempsey, Nikolai, Takeo and Samantha against Richtofen. Groph and Schuster appear through historical reels.

### Confirmed Lore

- **[Confirmed]** Griffin Station is a secret Group 935 Moon base built around the MPD and supplied with test subjects whose souls power it.[^16]
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen's plan succeeds in its immediate objective: he gains control of the undead. Maxis's countermeasure also succeeds physically—three missiles strike Earth—but fails to provide a clean victory, creating the ruined world inherited by Black Ops II.[^14][^15]
- **[Confirmed]** Later chronology identifies 2025 as the date of the Moon event, although the crew reached it through nonlinear travel.[^1][^15]

### Theories

- **[Theory]** Whether Maxis knowingly intended the full scale of Earth's destruction was debated after release. The observable result is confirmed; interpretations of his foresight and moral intent require later dialogue and remain character analysis.
- **[Theory]** Gameplay permits many delays between quest stages. Those rounds should not be read as a canonical duration for the operation.

## Black Ops synthesis

- **[Confirmed]** The five-map arc is a scavenger hunt with escalating consequences: recover the Vril Device, recover the Focusing Stone, combine them at the MPD, and seize control of the Aether.[^1][^8][^11][^14]
- **[Confirmed]** The storytelling format evolves during the game itself: Kino relies on reels; Ascension formalizes a cooperative quest; Call of the Dead adds a cinematic cast premise; Shangri-La makes temporal revision playable; Moon delivers a world-changing outcome in real time.
- **[Confirmed]** Richtofen wins the contest for the MPD, but Maxis turns that victory into a new apocalypse. This unresolved struggle directly frames the Victis maps in Black Ops II.
- **[Theory]** The maps support a coherent itinerary, but not every round, side song or random teleporter room has a fixed place in that itinerary. The quest outcomes are stronger canon than the mechanics of an arbitrary match.
